# Sanctions, Export Control, and Restricted Parties

**Version:** 1.0 · **Related:** PASVL Article 22

## 1. Why this document exists

A license grants rights. Those rights may not be exercised where doing so would violate export
control or sanctions law. Because the framework is international and its Registry is public,
the Steward must apply a consistent, documented screening process — and must be transparent
about its limits.

## 2. The obligations

### 2.1 For deployers

You must not exercise rights under the License, and must not deploy a Work, where:

- you are located in, organized under the laws of, or ordinarily resident in a comprehensively
  sanctioned territory;
- you are identified on a restricted-party list (for example, UN Security Council lists, the
  EU Consolidated List, the UK OFSI list, or the US SDN and Entity Lists, as applicable to
  you);
- the Work would be used in a prohibited end-use, including weapons of mass destruction,
  missiles, or certain military end-uses; or
- the transaction would be prohibited by your own jurisdiction's rules (for example,
  re-export controls).

### 2.2 For Authors and the Steward

Before issuing a LOSDA, the issuing authority must screen the applicant against at least:

1. the UN Security Council consolidated sanctions list;
2. the sanctions list of the applicant's and the issuer's jurisdictions (for the Steward, this
   includes Bangladesh's applicable measures and the lists of its partners);
3. lists maintained by the jurisdiction of the Work's distribution, where the Author
   designates them in the NOTICE.

Screening must be recorded (date, lists consulted, result) and retained for 5 years.

## 3. What screening cannot do

Screening is a reasonable-efforts control, not a guarantee. It cannot detect:

- a licensee acting as a front for a restricted party;
- a change of beneficial ownership after the LOSDA is issued;
- an end-use concealed from the issuer;
- a re-export by the licensee contrary to its own jurisdiction's rules.

Authors and the Steward therefore rely on contractual undertakings (PASVL §11.8, Article 22) and
on notification duties rather than on perfect knowledge.

## 4. Territories and self-certification

The Registry does not maintain a public blocklist of countries; such lists change frequently
and publishing a stale list is worse than publishing none. Instead:

- applicants self-certify compliance with this document;
- the Registry records the applicant's country;
- a LOSDA for a comprehensively sanctioned territory is not issued;
- a LOSDA already issued is suspended on a change of law (PASVL §18.7(e)).

## 5. Export classification

PASVL works are software. Most are not export-controlled. Some are. The Author is responsible
for:

- determining whether the Work is subject to export classification (for example, ECCN
  classification under the US EAR, or the equivalent under the Wassenaar Arrangement);
- stating any classification in the NOTICE;
- obtaining any required license before distributing the Work publicly;
- instructing the Steward if the Work must not be listed publicly in the Registry.

The Steward does not perform export classification for Authors. A LOSDA does not authorize any
export that the Author is not entitled to make.

## 6. Cryptographic software

Where the Work includes cryptography:

- the Author must state this in the NOTICE;
- the Author is responsible for any notification or registration requirements in its
  jurisdiction;
- deployers are responsible for their own jurisdiction's requirements (for example, import
  registration or use restrictions).

## 7. Change of circumstances

A licensee must notify the issuing authority within 15 days if it becomes subject to a
restricted-party listing, changes its country of organization, or becomes aware that its
deployment has a prohibited end-use. Failure to notify is a Violation.

## 8. Sanctions evasion

Deliberate structuring to evade sanctions — including through intermediaries, shell entities,
or relabelling — is a critical Violation. The Steward will revoke the LOSDA, record the action
(redacted to the extent required by law), and report to competent authorities where it is
lawful and appropriate to do so.

## 9. Humanitarianism and licensed exceptions

Where a general license, humanitarian exemption, or specific authorization from a competent
authority permits a transaction, the framework does not obstruct it. An applicant relying on an
exemption must state the legal basis in the request.

## 10. Record keeping

| Record | Retention |
|---|---|
| Screening result | 5 years from the end of the term |
| Sanctions-related revocations | Permanent (Registry) |
| Notifications of change of circumstance | 5 years |

## 11. Not legal advice

Export control is highly technical and jurisdiction-specific. This document is a policy
framework, not legal advice. Authors and deployers should consult qualified counsel.
