# Conflict of Interest Policy

**Version:** 1.0 · **Applies to:** License Council members, Registry Custodians, appeals and
reinstatement panellists, Ethics Review Board members, and any person acting for the Steward.

## 1. Principle

A decision-maker must not decide a matter in which they have a personal, financial, or
reputational interest that a reasonable observer would consider capable of influencing them. The
test is **apparent conflict**, not only actual bias.

## 2. What must be declared

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Financial | Employment, consultancy, equity, royalties, loans, or a licensing fee from a party |
| Commercial | Owning a competing product, or a contract with a party |
| Personal | Close family or household relationship with a party |
| Organisational | Board membership, advisory role, or volunteer leadership in a party |
| Litigation | Being a party or a witness in related proceedings |
| Prior involvement | Having made the original decision, advised on it, or investigated it |
| Gifts | Any benefit received within the last 12 months |
| Public advocacy | A published position specifically on the matter at hand |

## 3. Declaration procedure

1. Declare **before** the matter is discussed, not after.
2. Record the declaration in the conflict register with the date and the nature of the interest.
3. The chair decides whether the interest requires recusal.
4. If recusal is required: the person leaves the discussion, does not vote, and receives no
   confidential material.
5. If the chair is conflicted, the vice-chair decides.
6. If the majority of the body is conflicted, the matter is referred to the License Council, or
   to an independent panel if the Council is also conflicted.

## 4. The register

The conflict register records:

- the person's name and role;
- the date of the declaration;
- the nature of the interest, without unnecessary personal detail;
- the decision (no action / recusal / referred);
- the affected matter.

The register is available to the independent auditor and is summarised annually.

## 5. Standing declarations

Officers file an annual standing declaration covering all interests. New interests must be
declared within 14 days of arising. A person who fails to file a standing declaration may not
participate in decisions until they do.

## 6. Gifts and hospitality

See `../legal/anti-corruption.md`, Section 2. Declared gifts above USD 50 are recorded in the
same register.

## 7. Consequences of non-declaration

1. The decision may be set aside on appeal.
2. The person may be removed from their role.
3. The matter may be referred for external review.
4. In cases of deliberate concealment, the person becomes ineligible for any framework role.

## 8. Recusal does not block the process

A recusal must not be used to obstruct a decision. Where a conflicted member's participation is
necessary for quorum, the body must appoint an alternate rather than proceed with a conflicted
member.

## 9. Related-party transactions

Any contract between the Steward and an entity connected to an officer must be:

1. approved by the Council without the conflicted member present;
2. priced at market rates;
3. disclosed in the annual report.

## 10. Review

Reviewed annually by the License Council.
