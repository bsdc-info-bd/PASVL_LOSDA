# Ethics Review Board

**Version:** 1.0 · **Type:** Advisory body · **Appointed by:** License Council

## 1. Purpose

The Ethics Review Board advises on whether a proposed use falls within the prohibited uses in
`legal/ethics-policy.md` and whether a high-risk use requires additional review. It exists so
that difficult decisions are made by a group with relevant expertise, in public, with reasons —
not by a single person reacting to a headline.

## 2. Composition

- **Five to seven members**, serving three-year renewable terms.
- At least one member with expertise in human rights.
- At least one member with expertise in security or defence technology.
- At least one member with expertise in data protection.
- At least one member from a low- or middle-income country.
- At least one member independent of the software industry.

Members declare conflicts under `CONFLICT-OF-INTEREST.md`. A member with a direct interest in
the applicant or the use case must recuse.

## 3. Where the Board is involved

| Situation | Board role |
|---|---|
| A proposed use matches a high-risk domain | Advisory review before a LOSDA is issued |
| An alleged prohibited use is reported | Advisory opinion to the enforcement authority |
| A new prohibited use is proposed for the Ethics Policy | Mandatory consultation |
| A LOSDA is appealed on ethics grounds | Advisory opinion to the Appeals Panel |
| A government deployment is contested | Optional review at the Council's request |

The Board does not issue or revoke LOSDAs. It advises; the decision-maker decides and must
publish whether it followed the advice and why.

## 4. Review procedure

1. **Referral** — the issuer, an enforcement authority, or the Council refers a matter.
2. **Acknowledgment** — within 5 business days.
3. **Information gathering** — the applicant may submit a written statement and may be heard.
4. **Deliberation** — in closed session, with conflicts declared.
5. **Opinion** — within 30 business days, extended to 45 for complex cases.
6. **Publication** — the opinion, redacted for confidentiality, is published within 10 business
   days.

## 5. Substantive tests

### 5.1 Is the use prohibited?

Apply the prohibited-use list narrowly. If the use is not expressly prohibited, it is not
prohibited. Absence of a prohibition is not an endorsement.

### 5.2 Is the use high-risk?

Consider:

1. the severity of harm if the system fails;
2. the reversibility of the harm;
3. the vulnerability of the affected people;
4. the degree of human oversight;
5. the transparency and appeal mechanisms available to affected people;
6. the presence of independent audit;
7. whether the use is lawful in the deployer's jurisdiction.

### 5.3 Proportionality

A restriction must be the least intrusive means of addressing a real risk. A blanket ban on an
entire sector requires a strong justification.

## 6. Opinions

Opinions may be:

- **Permitted** — no prohibited use identified.
- **Permitted with conditions** — for example, an independent audit, human-in-the-loop, or a
  published impact assessment.
- **Requires further review** — the referrer must obtain specified evidence.
- **Not permitted** — the use falls within a prohibited category.

## 7. Transparency

The Board publishes:

- an annual report of referrals, categories, and outcomes;
- anonymised opinions where the parties consent;
- aggregate statistics on prohibited-use findings.

## 8. Independence

The Board may publish an opinion that disagrees with the Council. The Council may not remove a
member for the content of an opinion. Removal is only for conduct, conflict, or absence, and
requires a hearing.

## 9. Limits

1. The Board does not determine the legality of a use in any jurisdiction.
2. The Board does not provide legal advice to any party.
3. The Board does not review general-purpose software for every conceivable misuse.
4. The Board's opinions are not binding on a court or on an Author's private commercial
   judgment, except where the Ethics Policy is incorporated into a LOSDA.

## 10. Review

The Board's terms of reference are reviewed every two years.
