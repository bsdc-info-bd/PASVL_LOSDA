# Code of Conduct

**Version:** 1.0 · **Applies to:** all framework spaces — repositories, issues, pull requests,
discussions, chat, mailing lists, events, and any interaction made in the framework's name.

---

## 1. Our commitment

The framework exists to make software more transparent and more honestly paid for. That work
requires disagreement — about licensing, about law, about ethics, and about whether this whole
idea is a good one. It does not require contempt.

We commit to a community in which participation is safe for anyone who engages in good faith,
regardless of age, body size, caste, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal
appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

---

## 2. Expected behavior

1. **Argue with the argument.** Attack the license, the clause, the analysis — not the person.
2. **Assume good faith first.** Most people who disagree with you have a reason.
3. **Be precise.** "This clause is unenforceable in Germany because of §307 BGB" beats "this is
   nonsense".
4. **Give credit.** Cite the person whose idea you are building on, or arguing with.
5. **Accept correction.** If you are wrong, say so and move on. It costs nothing here.
6. **Respect privacy.** Do not publish someone's personal information, employment details, or
   private correspondence.
7. **Protect the vulnerable.** A licensee who is a sole trader, a student, or a small business is
   not a fair target in a dispute.
8. **Use the process.** If you believe a decision is wrong, appeal it. Do not run a campaign
   against a person.
9. **Keep it on topic.** Licensing disputes are not proxy battles for unrelated political fights.
10. **Remember the reader.** This repository is read by people with limited legal experience and
    limited English. Write so they can follow.

---

## 3. Unacceptable behavior

1. Harassment, intimidation, stalking, or following a person across spaces.
2. Discriminatory comments or jokes about protected characteristics.
3. Sexual attention or advances of any kind, in any space.
4. Threats of violence, or encouragement of violence.
5. Publishing private information without consent (doxxing).
6. Deliberate misrepresentation of another person's position, in order to make it easier to
   attack.
7. Retaliation against someone who reports a violation, participates in an enforcement process,
   or refuses to participate in a violation.
8. Spam, brigading, or coordinated disruption of discussions.
9. Legal threats used to silence discussion rather than to resolve a genuine dispute.
10. Knowingly false reports of license violations or code-of-conduct breaches.
11. Sustained disruption of a discussion after being asked to stop.
12. Advocating or encouraging any of the above.

---

## 4. Scope

This code applies in all framework spaces, and when an individual is representing the framework
in public — for example, as a Council member, an issuer, a panellist, a speaker, or a moderator.

It also applies to conduct outside framework spaces where that conduct has a direct and material
effect on the safety of framework participants.

---

## 5. Reporting

### 5.1 Where

Send a report to the conduct contact published in [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md). If the report
concerns the conduct contact, send it to the License Council's integrity contact.

### 5.2 What to include

- What happened, and where.
- When it happened.
- Who was involved (names or handles).
- Any evidence (screenshots, links, messages).
- Whether you want confidentiality.
- What outcome you are seeking.

### 5.3 Confidentiality

Reports are handled confidentially. The identity of a reporter is not disclosed to the accused
without consent, except where disclosure is necessary to investigate fairly or is required by
law. If disclosure is necessary and you do not consent, we will explain the consequence and let
you decide whether to proceed.

### 5.4 Timelines

| Step | Target |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgment | 3 business days |
| Initial assessment | 10 business days |
| Investigation (if needed) | 30 business days |
| Decision and communication | 45 business days |

## 6. Enforcement

Sanctions are graduated:

| Level | Sanction | Typical for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Private correction and apology | A first, minor, inadvertent breach |
| 2 | Formal warning, with conditions | Repeated minor breaches |
| 3 | Temporary restriction from a space (7–90 days) | Harassment, sustained disruption |
| 4 | Permanent ban from framework spaces | Serious harassment, threats, doxxing |
| 5 | Removal from a framework role; referral to authorities | Violence threats, criminal conduct |

**Where the person holds a framework role** (Council member, Custodian, panellist, moderator),
removal from the role is available at Level 3 and above and requires a hearing.

**Should a sanction affect a LOSDA?** No. A code-of-conduct sanction is not a licensing sanction.
Only the grounds in PASVL §18.7 can affect a LOSDA. Conduct that is also a license violation may
be pursued separately under the Enforcement Policy.

---

## 7. Fairness

1. The accused is told the substance of the allegation and given a chance to respond.
2. The decision-maker must not have a conflict of interest.
3. The decision and reasons are communicated to both parties.
4. Sanctions are recorded in a confidential register, summarized in the annual transparency report.
5. A sanction of Level 2 or above may be appealed within 20 business days to a person who did not
   make the decision.

---

## 8. Good-faith reports

A report made in good faith, which turns out not to be substantiated, is not a breach of this
code. A report made knowingly in bad faith is a breach.

---

## 9. Attribution

This code is informed by the Contributor Covenant (v2.1) and by the experience of communities
that have learned — sometimes painfully — what happens when conduct standards are not written
down. It is adapted for a legal framework, where disputes are expected and must remain
professional.

---

## 10. Review

Reviewed annually by the License Council with input from the community.
